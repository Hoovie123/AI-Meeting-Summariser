Good evening everyone, and thank you all for joining us tonight for this annual technology
showcase.
Over the past ten years, we have seen artificial intelligence evolve from massive supercomputer
into tools at every day people use on a daily basis.
Yet for most of that time, running smart voice tools required sending your private audio
across the Internet to distant cloud-mator centres.
At cloud-first approach meant paying expensive monthly subscription fees just to transcribe a
simple lecture or team meeting.
It also meant that whenever your Internet connection dropped out in a classroom, train, or
workshop, your productivity tools stopped working completely.
Even more importantly, uploading sensitive conversations to third-party service forced
people to give up ownership and privacy over their own spoken words.
Today, we are witnessing a major shift back toward local, offline computing powered by compact
open source models.
Thanks to modern model compression and quantized speech recognition, tasks that once required
an entire server rack are unquietly on a single credit card-sized computer.
When your speech detects engine and language model unlocally on your own desk, your microphone
or the owner leaves the room.
There are no API-rate limits, no surprise cloud bills at the end of the month, and zero reliance
on external companies keeping their servers online.
Whether you are a student recording university seminar, an engineer-logging workshop notes, or
a small business summarizing client calls, local hardware puts you in total control.
Of course, building dependable software on resource-constrained boards teaches valuable engineering
discipline.
When you only have one gigabyte of memory and four modest processor cores, every single
megabyte of RAM, and every background thread must be managed carefully.
You learn how to unload models cleanly with windteps, keep prompts focused, and stream
output in real-time solar system feels responsive.
Rather than hiding behind limitless cloud hardware, working within tight hardware boundaries
forces you to understand how software truly operates under the hood.
Looking ahead over the next few years, edge processors, and small-language models will continue
to become dramatically faster and more capable.
The skills you develop right now by building offline voice pipelines on tiny boards will
scale directly to the next generation of personal devices.
I encourage everyone here tonight to keep tinkering, keep testing new ideas, and share what
you build with the wider open source community.
Never underestimate what a small, affordable computer can achieve when paired with clean
code, and thoughtful design.
Thank you very much for your time tonight, and I wish you all the best with your upcoming
projects.
